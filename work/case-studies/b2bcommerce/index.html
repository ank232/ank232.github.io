Pricing Architecture Modernisation

Modernising Salesforce B2B Commerce pricing by replacing a complex 26-price-book architecture with a scalable account-driven pricing model.

Executive Summary

As product catalogues grow, pricing strategies often become increasingly complex. In this implementation, a wellness products company using Salesforce B2B Commerce had evolved to manage personalised customer pricing through twenty-six separate price books. While the approach achieved the required pricing flexibility, it introduced significant operational overhead whenever products were added or existing pricing changed. I redesigned the pricing architecture by extending Salesforce B2B Commerce's native pricing engine, replacing the configuration-heavy model with a scalable account-based pricing solution. The new approach reduced the pricing model from twenty-six price books to three category-specific base price books while continuing to deliver personalised pricing to every customer across the storefront. Rather than replacing Salesforce Commerce functionality, the solution leveraged the platform's standard pricing framework and extended it where necessary, resulting in a cleaner, more maintainable architecture that continues to operate in production.

The Challenge

The organisation operates an Aura-based Salesforce B2B Commerce storefront serving different categories of customers, each receiving negotiated pricing based on their commercial relationship. Historically, personalised pricing was achieved by maintaining twenty-six separate price books, with each customer assigned to a dedicated pricing structure. Although functionally correct, the model became increasingly difficult to maintain as the product catalogue expanded. Every new product introduction required administrators to update multiple price books individually. Product maintenance became repetitive, pricing administration became increasingly error-prone, and introducing catalogue changes required significantly more effort than necessary. The objective was not simply to reduce the number of price books. The new solution needed to preserve personalised pricing for every customer while remaining fully compatible with Salesforce B2B Commerce's native pricing pipeline.

The Solution

Instead of maintaining individual price books for every customer, I redesigned the pricing architecture around customer categories. Three base price books were introduced to represent the primary pricing models: Retail, Wholesale and COG. Each product now exists only within the appropriate pricing category. Products that are exclusive to a particular customer segment naturally remain available only through their corresponding price book, eliminating unnecessary duplication across multiple pricing structures. Customer-specific pricing is no longer maintained through separate price books. Instead, account managers define percentage discounts directly on Account records using custom Salesforce fields. These values represent negotiated pricing for each customer category and can be managed without modifying product pricing data. Buyer Groups determine the appropriate base pricing, while a custom extension of Salesforce B2B Commerce's PricingService applies account-specific discounts dynamically during price calculation. The result is personalised pricing that behaves consistently across the storefront while dramatically simplifying ongoing catalogue maintenance.

Architecture Overview

The solution extends Salesforce B2B Commerce's standard pricing lifecycle rather than replacing it. Each authenticated customer belongs to a Buyer Group that determines the appropriate pricing category. The Buyer Group provides the corresponding base price from one of the three category-specific price books. During price calculation, the custom PricingService retrieves the customer's negotiated discount percentages stored on the Account record. The service calculates the final selling price by applying the configured percentage discount to the base catalogue price before returning the personalised value to the storefront. Because the implementation integrates directly into Salesforce Commerce's native pricing framework, personalised prices remain consistent throughout the customer's purchasing journey, including product listings, product detail pages, the shopping cart and checkout.

Technical Implementation

Phase 1 · Simplifying the Pricing Model

The first stage involved analysing the existing pricing strategy and identifying common pricing patterns across the customer base. Rather than maintaining separate price books for individual accounts, pricing was normalised into three category-specific base price books representing Retail, Wholesale and COG customers. This significantly reduced pricing duplication while preserving flexibility for future catalogue growth.

Phase 2 · Account-Based Pricing

Customer-specific discounts were moved from pricing configuration into business data. Custom fields were introduced on the Account object, allowing sales managers to configure negotiated percentage discounts independently for each pricing category. This separated catalogue pricing from commercial agreements, making ongoing pricing administration considerably simpler.

Phase 3 · Extending the Native Pricing Engine

Salesforce B2B Commerce's PricingService was extended to introduce personalised pricing while preserving the platform's standard pricing workflow. During each pricing request, the service retrieves the customer's applicable base price and calculates the final selling price using the negotiated discount stored on the Account. By extending the native pricing engine instead of replacing it, the implementation remains aligned with Salesforce's Commerce architecture and benefits from the existing pricing lifecycle.

Phase 4 · Consistent Storefront Experience

The pricing service is invoked throughout the storefront, ensuring customers see identical personalised pricing regardless of where products appear. This provides a seamless purchasing experience while requiring no manual pricing adjustments across different storefront components.

Buyer Group Routing

Centralised Price Logic

Platform First

Built on native Commerce capabilities instead of replacing them, improving maintainability and long-term scalability.

Engineering Challenges

The primary challenge was simplifying a mature pricing architecture without changing how customers experienced pricing on the storefront. Every pricing calculation needed to remain accurate while replacing an implementation that had evolved over time around numerous individual price books. Careful consideration was required to ensure personalised pricing continued to behave consistently across product discovery, cart management and checkout. Another important consideration was preserving compatibility with Salesforce B2B Commerce's native pricing lifecycle. Rather than introducing parallel pricing logic, the implementation extended the existing platform capabilities, allowing future platform enhancements and commerce features to continue operating within the standard pricing framework.
